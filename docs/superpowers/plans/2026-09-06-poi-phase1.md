# Points of interest, phase 1 — the two mechanisms, buttes and the arch

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** both POI mechanisms landed against the simplest shape of each kind —
**desert buttes** as a masked landform inside `Noise.height`, and the **natural
stone arch** as a resolved site stamped per chunk. Design:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-06-points-of-interest-design.md`.

**Architecture:** landform terms live in `CubeForge.Noise`, beside the dune and
glacier masks, because that is what they are — fields, evaluated per column,
inside `height`. `CubeForge.Poi` holds the structures: hashed sites on their own
cell grid, resolved and stamped once per chunk, exactly as `Trees` places trees.
`Poi` depends on `Noise` and `Chunk`; nothing depends on `Poi` but the one
`stamp` call at the end of `Chunk.generate_lakes`. No value is threaded and no
existing signature changes.

**Tech Stack:** March. No C change in this phase (see Task 6).

## Why the butte top is an absolute level

A mesa whose rise is a constant added to the ground has a top that follows the
ground, so on any slope it comes out tilted — which no butte is. Instead each
cell hashes an **absolute top level**, and the rise is `top - ground`. A cell
whose ground is already within 8 of its top holds nothing. That makes the tops
level without probing another column (which a landform may not do), and it makes
buttes erosional remnants of a former surface, which is what they are.

### Task 5 — BUILT 2026-09-06, ahead of phase 2: the water depth tint

**The problem.** A lagoon 24 blocks deep renders exactly like a puddle. A water
fragment's colour is its texture times the light, with alpha from the texture;
nothing in the pipeline knows how much water is under the surface. The atoll is
the feature that makes this matter, but the sea shelf and every lake want it too.

**Rejected, with reasons.** Sampling the depth buffer needs an FBO or a copy, and
this renderer deliberately has none — the shadows are a voxel DDA for exactly
that reason. A tenth vertex float would cost ~11% more vertex memory on every
vertex in the world to carry a water-only fact (the layout has gone 7 -> 8 -> 9
already, for light and for weather). Marching `u_occ` down from the fragment
fails because occupancy stores opacity, not identity: water and any other
translucent block are the same value there.

**Chosen: the still-water effect's spare byte.** The `fx` word is
`effect * 256 + alpha`, and for water effects the low byte carries flow speed
rather than alpha. Effect 10 is *still* water, whose speed is zero by definition
and whose low byte the fragment shader never reads — `spd` is used only for
effects 2..9. So the mesher writes the **depth of water below the cell**, in
blocks, into that byte for still-water faces. No layout change, no new uniform,
no new pass, nothing costs anything anywhere else.

- Mesher: count water blocks below the cell (the mesher has the chunk),
  **quantised to eight steps of four blocks** and capped at 32. The quantisation
  matters: the fx word is part of the greedy key, so unquantised depth would
  stop a flat lagoon floor merging at all. Water vertex count before and after
  is the measurement that decides the step size.
- Shader, before the fog mix:
  ```
  const vec3 DEEP = vec3(0.05, 0.22, 0.40);
  float wd = (fe == 10) ? float(fxw & 255) * (32.0 / 255.0) : 0.0;
  float f  = 1.0 - exp(-wd * 0.16);
  lit = mix(lit, DEEP * (0.35 + 0.65 * sk), f);
  a   = mix(t.a * a, 1.0, 0.7 * f);
  ```
  `sk` keeps a flooded cave dark instead of glowing blue.
- Verification: the sea shelf at seed 7 shows a gradient from the shore outward;
  a one-block puddle is pixel-identical; water vertices before and after; the
  frame-30 mesh hash moves **once**, deliberately, and is re-baselined in
  `RESULTS.md` with the reason.

### Task 6 — BUILT 2026-09-06, as effect 12: coloured crystal glow

Built early, because the crystal cavern is what needed it: a near-white selenite
beam lit by the one warm block-light channel rendered beige, and no texture
change could fix that. See RESULTS. The flag reaches the shim on the layer table
(`Texture.selflit_bias`) rather than through a second FFI array.

Block light is one channel and `GLOW` is a warm constant, so a blue crystal would
light itself orange. Three block-light fields would fix it properly and are far
out of scope. The cheap nine-tenths: a new effect id **12, "self-lit"**, on an
emissive block's own faces, and one line in the fragment shader —

```
vec3 glow = (fe == 12) ? t.rgb : GLOW;
vec3 world = max(baked, bl * glow) + vec3(flash);
```

— so an emissive block's glow takes its colour from its own texture. The light it
**casts** on the surrounding rock stays warm; that limit is real and goes in the
spec rather than being papered over. One line in the mesher (the fx word for the
crystal ids) and one in the shader.
